# Flow control: current implementation

This describes general3, verified by release and ASAN/UBSAN integration checks. The previous `4b9ea36` test and benchmark results do not validate this implementation. Wire format remains NSL1 version 1; changed frame size and concurrency semantics do not promise compatibility with older viewers.

## Ownership and bounds

The service thread owns `FlowControl` state and socket writes; its mutex protects state shared with the capture publication callback. The capture worker owns its `CaptureSource` and codec and publishes owning `EncodedFrame` vectors; see [backend boundaries](backends.md). The browser retains pending decode records and any decoded frames until it can draw them in sequence. The service counts pending and sent encoded access units together: at most eight frames and 8,388,608 encoded payload bytes total, with an individual maximum of 8,388,608 bytes. Exceeding the bound rejects publication and fails the generation instead of dropping a reference frame. The viewer applies the same count and encoded-byte limits. These are logical payload limits, not RSS limits. Up to 32 MiB of additional transient buffers is an estimate, not a measured or enforced total; libwebsockets, codec, browser, allocator and other allocations are separate.

The first frame uses a window of one. After each accepted ACK, the exponentially weighted ACK turnaround is `0.875 * old + 0.125 * sample` (first sample initializes it), in microseconds. The effective frame window is `clamp(ceil(ack_rtt_us / 16666.667) + 1, 2, 8)`. ACK turnaround runs from server send to received ACK and includes network, decode, draw and scheduling; it is not pure network RTT or physical presentation latency. ACKs are accepted only for the sent front sequence within the current generation; duplicates, future and stale ACKs do not grant credit. The viewer buffers decoded output as necessary, draws in sequence, ACKs after draw and closes frames.

Capture admission pauses when the oldest outstanding capture reaches 250 ms or logical bytes reach 8 MiB. The worker also receives an admission deadline based on the oldest capture timestamp. This is not a hard bound on displayed frame age: work already admitted or in transit can take longer. A generation ends if the oldest capture is at least three seconds old or no ACK progress occurs for three seconds while frames remain outstanding. The service checks on a 100 ms timer, subject to scheduling delay. Failure, disconnect or timeout releases held controls; reconnect starts a new generation with an IDR and parameter sets. The reference chain is never intentionally advanced by dropping a queued encoded frame.

## Rate feedback

Encoder CRF is 10; initial `rc_max_rate` is 8,000,000 bits/s and `rc_buffer_size` 2,000,000 bits. The worker sets maximum rate from the target and buffer size to target divided by four, a nominal 250 ms VBV buffer. Target is clamped to 1,000,000–40,000,000 bits/s. The independent rate-control probe verified 8→2→8 Mbps reconfiguration with 720 decoded frames against the installed codec. End-to-end quality is measured separately; target is not a strict instantaneous wire-rate or quality guarantee.

A continuous-demand sample resets when consecutive sent-frame times differ by more than 50 ms. A sample is busy after at least eight ACKs and 65,536 bytes; after 500 ms, goodput is acknowledged payload bytes times eight divided by elapsed seconds. During outstanding congestion, a decrease requires at least two frames, outstanding bytes at least the smaller of target bitrate divided by 80 and 65,536 bytes, oldest capture age at least 200 ms (80% of the 250 ms admission budget), and 500 ms since the last decrease. It sets target to 75% of its previous value, additionally limited to 90% of sampled goodput when a busy sample of at least 500 ms exists, then clamps at 1 Mbps. It also resets the increase timer.

An increase requires a busy sample of at least one second, oldest age below 100 ms, at least one second since the last increase and two seconds since the last decrease. The target increases by 10%, capped at 40 Mbps. Sample counters reset when the sampling interval reaches one second. Sparse or idle traffic does not establish link capacity. These heuristics are implementation details, not verified congestion-control or fidelity guarantees. There is no quiet-period refinement or enforced visual quality floor; 4:2:0 can lose colored-text detail even at CRF 10.

## Capture interface and observability

`CaptureEncoder::setFlowControl(uint32_t generation, unsigned window, uint64_t bitrate, bool paused, uint64_t admit_until_us)` updates worker admission under its mutex, ignores zero or mismatched generations, clamps window to 1–8 and bitrate to the range above, and wakes the worker. `admit_until_us` is an absolute server steady-clock deadline in microseconds; zero disables that deadline. `acknowledge(generation, sequence)` advances only the next previously encoded sequence in the active generation. Publication transfers ownership of each encoded vector to transport; the codec remains worker-owned.

`GET /metrics` adds `inflight_frames`, `inflight_bytes`, `window_frames`, `target_bitrate_bps`, `ack_rtt_ms`, `oldest_frame_ms`, `frames_acked`, `frames_encoded`, `observed_busy_goodput_bps`, and the configured `max_inflight_frames`, `max_inflight_bytes`, `max_frame_bytes`, `admission_age_ms`, `no_ack_timeout_ms`, `max_frame_age_ms`. Flow counts and samples reset with the generation. Browser `window.nativeStats` exposes `received`, `drawn`, `bytes`, `decodeerrors`, `outstanding`, `pendingBytes`, and `pendingHighWater`; these are not physical-presentation metrics. Existing cgroup metrics cover the mounted cgroup and exclude the browser; unavailable counters are null and do not stop transport metrics. The default listener is loopback. Authenticated remote access uses the [portable deployment contract](portable.md).

The decrease threshold uses an absolute age budget because smoothed ACK turnaround includes queueing delay: letting that delay raise its own threshold masked congestion. Capping the byte guard prevents a high encoder ceiling from masking an already meaningful backlog. Paths with irreducible latency near or above 200 ms can repeatedly back off; this policy does not claim optimal behavior on arbitrary long-RTT paths. Recovery may change quality as it probes, and the benchmark reports those tradeoffs.
