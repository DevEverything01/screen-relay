# General-engine architecture review

Scope: source review of native core `4b9ea36` and its current contract. This is not a new benchmark, a claim of general superiority, or evidence that a proposed change works. The qualification plan governs the independent comparisons and holdout.

## Evidence and limitations

The [confirmed summary](../.context/compound-engineering/ce-optimize/native-stream/confirmed-summary.json) reports all original gates passed for this commit. In its narrow local 720p small-region benchmark, native median motion was 52.91 visible updates/s, 43.2 ms p95 latency, 41.31% CPU, 155.44 MiB working-set approximation, and 1.29 Mbps; PSNR was 38.45 dB and text ink MAE 4.10. Selkies measured 54.54 updates/s and Kasm 46.85 updates/s on that motion case. These results do not establish WAN behavior, full-screen motion cost, broad text fidelity, physical display latency, or overall feature parity. The qualification plan lists the missing cases.

## Structural findings, in priority order

1. **One decoded-and-drawn ACK gates every capture.** [Capture admission](../../../src/core/capture.cpp#L218) requires credit and [clears it for every frame](../../../src/core/capture.cpp#L277); [server](../../../src/server/server.cpp#L153) and [viewer](../../../viewer/viewer.js#L210) enforce one outstanding frame, ACKing only [after canvas draw](../../../viewer/viewer.js#L235). For continuous damage, the theoretical ceiling is at most `min(60, 1 / (RTT + capture + encode + transmit serialization + decode + draw + scheduling))` frames/s, with non-overlapping components expressed in seconds. With 50 ms RTT the ideal ceiling is 20 fps; at 100 ms it is 10 fps, before other delay. These are derived limits, not measurements. ACK means canvas draw, not physical presentation.
2. **No configured bandwidth budget or congestion control.** [Encoder settings](../../../src/core/capture.cpp#L95) fix ultrafast CRF 10 with no explicit bitrate/VBV limit. Frame size varies with content; full-frame high motion may exceed link capacity, increasing frame age or triggering the [three-second ACK timeout](../../../src/server/server.cpp#L316). This is a risk to measure, not proof that a specific link will fail. An IDR on reconnect may itself be large. WebSocket uses ordered reliable delivery; slow delivery or loss can hold later data behind earlier bytes. Input and frames share a connection, but they travel in opposite directions, so a downstream frame alone does not establish upstream input head-of-line blocking; test contention and event-loop effects rather than assume it.
3. **Colored-text information is discarded before encoding.** [YUV420 selection](../../../src/core/capture.cpp#L95) and [2×2 chroma averaging](../../../src/core/color.cpp#L70) remove fine chroma detail. Increasing bitrate or lowering CRF cannot reconstruct discarded color differences. The existing single text score and static PSNR do not prove colored-edge fidelity or motion quality.
4. **Damage is a boolean, not a regional work budget.** [Damage events](../../../src/core/capture.cpp#L203) merely set `dirty`; each admitted update captures the entire root and converts the entire 1280×720 surface. Tiny updates therefore still pay full-frame capture/conversion cost. Regional work is a candidate only if profiling shows a benefit and correctness can be maintained for changed regions and encoder references.
5. **Memory is bounded per frame but copies and decoder queues matter.** Encoded packets accumulate in a vector, then [another full wire buffer](../../../src/server/server.cpp#L163) is constructed; browser and codec allocations add further memory. A single access unit can reach 16 MiB. The original working-set metric covers the container and is not peak per-process or browser memory. Any pipeline expansion needs explicit byte, count, and age bounds and peak measurement, including a slow receiver.
6. **Desktop product scope is narrow.** [README](../../../README.md) identifies fixed 720p, one local viewer, no authentication/TLS, audio, clipboard, resize, IME/full keyboard-layout support or relative pointer. The localhost Origin check is not authentication. Cursor fidelity also remains a contract limitation. These omissions prevent an overall replacement claim irrespective of benchmark speed; prioritize actual required capabilities before extending deployment beyond loopback.

## Decisions and minimum adaptive direction

- Replace stop-and-wait only with a bounded in-flight policy across capture, transport, viewer and ACK protocol. Bound bytes, count, frame age and decoder backlog; retain ordered reference dependencies. Drop/coalesce work before encoding where safe; do not discard arbitrary encoded reference frames. Define timeout, generation changes, stale ACKs, reconnect and decoder recovery together. The minimum viable window and recovery policy require measurement and a protocol decision.
- Define an observable bandwidth/freshness/fidelity contract before adding rate adaptation. Use measured feedback and explicit bounds; record chosen rate, quantizer/quality indicators, frame age and whether the quality floor is unmet. If the link cannot carry the floor, surface degraded or unavailable service rather than silently claiming equivalent quality. Do not add content-specific switches. Decide how rate and frame cadence are controlled and how fast they recover after bandwidth returns.
- Decide the minimum acceptable colored-text fidelity and browser-compatible encoding path. Higher chroma fidelity, a lossless or regional path, and their bandwidth/decoder costs are architectural tradeoffs, not mechanical CRF changes. Measure the same quality targets for all competitors and report quality alongside throughput.
- After profiling, remove demonstrably unnecessary copies or improve full-frame processing without changing pixel, ordering or recovery behavior. Instrumentation and copy reductions can be mechanical; partial capture, alternative transports or codec changes affect ownership and protocol contracts and need design and tests.
- Scope desktop features and secure remote access separately; never expose this loopback-only server as a WAN product by simply binding a public port.

## Acceptance tests

Keep one adaptive policy and comparable application, browser and resource limits across the workload matrix in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md). Establish thresholds from baseline measurements and explicit quality goals before selecting a winner; retain every failure and compare independent runs. Reserve a fresh holdout after changes.

- Sweep added RTT (including 0, 50 and 100 ms) and constrained bandwidth separately and together with continuous damage, realistic loss where available, and a slow decoder. Measure producer rate, decoded useful updates, frame age, p50/p95/p99 input-to-decoded-pixel latency, wire bytes, CPU, steady and peak memory, queue depth, stalls and reconnects. Distinguish simulated transport from physical WAN. Verify bounded queues/age and eventual delivery of the latest state after congestion clears.
- Exercise dense monochrome and colored text, scrolling, window movement, full-frame synthetic and natural video, mixed animation/text, and live game input. Compare pixel fidelity on static and moving frames, including colored glyph edges and local crops, not just global PSNR. Report quality and update rate together; no silent quality downgrade or workload-specific configuration.
- Force bandwidth below the chosen quality floor, then restore it; verify declared degradation, bounded resources, responsiveness and recovery without reconnect loops. Include large IDR transmission and timeout boundaries.
- Verify ordered reference decoding across multiple in-flight frames, safe coalescing, stale/future ACKs, reconnect and generation changes, malformed inputs, decoder stall, disconnect and held-control release. Test that no frame loss corrupts subsequent displayed content and that late frames do not regress displayed state.
- Test claimed desktop capabilities individually, including cursor, keyboard layouts, resize, clipboard, audio and secure remote access, only when implemented. A passing streaming benchmark cannot substitute for these functional tests.

## Concrete implementation sequence for a bounded experiment

This is a proposal, not an implemented change or a promised performance win. Freeze the workload-independent policy and thresholds before evaluating it against the breadth matrix. Retain the existing codec and dependencies, and run correctness gates before comparison.

1. **Instrument first.** In `src/server.cpp`, track generation-scoped send time, byte size and ACK time per sequence, in-flight bytes and frames, oldest age, and timeout/recovery reasons. In `viewer/viewer.js`, expose decode queue depth, draw count and decoder failures; retain the ACK definition as drawn, not presented. Use server monotonic timestamps for ACK turnaround; do not subtract browser and server clocks. Extend metrics tests and `tests/live-metrics-README.md` for units and unavailable fields. Establish baseline including slow-receiver memory and wire usage before tuning.
2. **Bounded window and reference safety.** In `src/capture.hpp` and `src/capture.cpp`, replace single credit/outstanding with worker-owned bounded credits, retaining a single dirty latch so unavailable capacity coalesces raw updates before encoding. In `src/server.cpp`, replace the sole `outstanding` with ordered generation/sequence records and bounded pending frames plus bytes. In `viewer/viewer.js`, replace the `pending.size` prohibition with an ordered, bounded timestamp-to-sequence map and ACK each drawn frame. Update `docs/build-contract.md` and integration fixtures together; duplicate, stale-generation and future/unsent ACKs must not free credit. Begin with hard frame-count and byte ceilings and an effective window based on measured ACK turnaround and target cadence; clamp it and change slowly. A window larger than the bandwidth-delay product is not a latency solution. Set explicit age and memory ceilings from qualification.

   Never drop an encoded reference frame and continue emitting dependent frames. When a queue/age limit or write/decode failure makes the chain unusable, stop admission, invalidate that generation, release controls, discard only after invalidation, and reconnect with a fresh IDR and headers. A slow receiver may make progress at reduced capture cadence; if no progress crosses a defined deadline, report the stall and reset. Validate that the first IDR fits the byte budget or fail visibly; avoid repeated unbounded IDR generation. Avoid per-frame decoder flushes. Test simultaneous, duplicate, stale and future ACKs, lost ACK, slow decoder, generation rollover, mid-chain disconnect, bounded byte/count/age, correct pixels after recovery, and cleanup of every frame and held input.
3. **Feedback-driven rate policy.** In `src/capture.cpp`, pass a clamped target from service-thread observations to the encoder worker via existing synchronization; only that worker touches the encoder context. Derive a conservative target from delivered bytes over ACK intervals, outstanding-byte age and observed progress, with hysteresis and bounded step sizes; initial target and ceilings must be explicit and measurable. Do not interpret an idle stream as zero network capacity, or claim a single frame ACK measures available bandwidth. Apply backpressure and capture cadence reduction when demand exceeds capacity. Add rate-target, observed goodput, frame-age and quality/degradation telemetry; test step-down, step-up, fluctuating capacity, long idle followed by motion, and missing feedback. Check quality collapse and oscillation as well as responsiveness.

   FFmpeg 7's [libx264 wrapper source](https://ffmpeg.org/doxygen/7.0/libx264_8c_source.html), located via Context7, shows per-frame reconfiguration from `AVCodecContext::rc_buffer_size` and `rc_max_rate`, and from `bit_rate` when x264 is already in ABR mode. Current CRF 10 is not ABR, so changing `bit_rate` alone is not an ABR control. Verify the installed FFmpeg/x264 build, initial rate-control mode, allowed transitions, units and actual output response in an isolated probe before choosing CRF+VBV or ABR+VBV. Do not assume a runtime mode change succeeds or nominal VBV settings guarantee a strict per-frame byte cap. If runtime reconfiguration is ineffective or unsupported, use bounded, infrequent encoder reopen with a forced IDR and account for recovery cost; test failed reconfiguration/reopen without dropping dependent frames.
4. **Quality floor and static refinement.** Establish fidelity thresholds from representative source/decoded crops before changing settings; include colored glyph edges, moving and static detail. Record target, measured quality and time to settle. For the baseline H.264/4:2:0 path, use a general quiet-period signal (no new damage, pending final source state) to request a final high-quality refresh within rate and age budgets; test that one final update arrives even if no later damage occurs. Its encoder control and reference-safe IDR/reconfiguration behavior must be proven on the installed build; re-encoding 4:2:0 cannot restore discarded chroma. If the network or codec cannot meet the declared floor, expose that state rather than silently reporting success. Test perpetual motion, a single final update, repeated brief quiet periods, no idle traffic after convergence, and restored-link recovery.

A 4:4:4-capable browser path or lossless image rectangles could address colored text beyond 4:2:0, but either adds a format/protocol and compatibility decision. Treat it as a separate optional phase after measuring the common pipeline; image rectangles must define compositing, ordering, damage, late arrivals, byte limits and interoperability. No text-specific mode or content classifier is part of the proposed baseline.
